Approach 1 · Content-based

It only ever read the film, never the crowd

Each film becomes a bag of weighted terms — genres ×3, community tags ×2, TMDB keywords ×2, director ×2, cast. Your ratings collapse into a single taste vector, and every unseen film is scored by cosine similarity against it. No other viewer is consulted at any point.

Your taste vector

The strongest dimensions of the profile, built from 232 ratings. Weight is how far a term sticks out after TF-IDF and L2 normalisation.

Approach 2 · Collaborative

It never once looked at what a film is about

No genres, no cast, no keywords — only the pattern of who rated what. Films become similar because the same people liked them. This is where genuine surprises come from, and it is also why a brand-new film is invisible to it.

The neighbourhood, in orbit

The target film sits at the centre. Each film you rated orbits at a radius of 1 − similarity, and its size is the weight it carries in the prediction. The sum of those weighted deviations is the recommendation.

radius = 1 − sim · mass = |sim × dev|

Which one is best? It depends what you ask

Every model is retrained on the same 80% training split and scored on ratings it has never seen — the newest 20% of each viewer's history. A temporal split, not a random one: predicting a viewer's past from their future would be cheating.

Read the table sideways, not down

The hybrid beats both of its own parents. That is the whole argument.

Content + item-kNN scores 0.0904 Prec@10 against 0.0840 for item-kNN alone and 0.0228 for content alone. SVD has the best RMSE of any single model yet ranks worse than item-kNN, which in turn predicts ratings worse than the trivial bias baseline. Optimising one does not give you the other.
